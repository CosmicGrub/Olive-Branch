# Corrections log: who-to-call guide, revision 2

Each verifier correction (VERIFY-who-to-call.md) was re-checked against raw page text (curl plus HTML strip, or pdftotext), 2026-11-14.

## Applied

- C1 US poison line: confirmed in the NCPC press release (number "still works", calls re-routed to neighbouring centres), MedlinePlus and America's Poison Centers (53 accredited centres, 24/7). Unresolved flag closed. The fallback "if it does not connect, call 911 if unwell or worried" rests only on the AAP line "if you believe your child is in danger, call 911"; no poison-line page describes a failed connection, and this is stated in the guide. The verifier's "nurse, pharmacist or doctor" was not carried (HRSA page not opened).
- C2 Lone rescuer: with a phone, call on speaker then CPR is confirmed by three opened authorities (AHA Heartsaver 2025 summary, St John Ambulance primary survey, American Red Cross child and baby CPR page, which lists the 9-1-1 call before the steps). Written as the default. Without a phone the opened authorities genuinely differ (1 minute: St John, British Red Cross; about 2 minutes: AHA, MedlinePlus), so it stays an explicit disagreement and is the top clinician question. The verifier's snippet-based claim that AHA and Red Cross say "2 minutes first" for an unwitnessed child was NOT confirmed: the Red Cross page's 2-minute line is worded for a baby, and the AHA summary says 2 minutes only when alone with no phone.
- C3 Region wording for how CPR starts (US compressions, UK 5 breaths), no pulse check (MedlinePlus).
- C4 Burn: 20 minutes carried as the most cautious (NHS x2, AAP thermal-burn photo caption), with 5 (minor burn only), 10, 20 and 20 to 30 shown. "Do not delay for clothing" is in an AAP caption; NHS says remove clothing near the burn unless stuck. Both carried. Cling film from NHS.
- C5 Anaphylaxis: AAP "never use other medicine in place of epinephrine", "ask for an ambulance with epinephrine", "tell rescue squad when last given", NHS "say you think it is anaphylaxis", "do not stand or walk" all confirmed raw. Positioning disagreement shown (AAP side-lying, NHS sit up slowly). No doses.
- C6 UK poison row rewritten to the NHS wording (confirmed raw); NHS accident page also says 999 for swallowed poison or too much medicine.
- C7 Neck injury: jaw lift confirmed in St John spinal page and MedlinePlus; "you may have to move them" removed; two-person move kept from MedlinePlus.
- C8 Canada: SickKids says poison information centre or 911, not 911 alone as the verifier summarised; both carried.
- C9 Deleted "questions do not delay" and "do not wait to find a parent, doctor or form".
- C10 Baby fever row moved to the ER list; disagreement kept. Confirmed raw: AAP "Is it a medical emergency" lists fever above 100.4 F under 60 days.
- C11 Consent: raw AAP page confirms it is addressed to practices and concerns non-urgent visits. "Leave written permission" is now labelled general practice, unsourced.
- C12 Not-verified items moved out of advice arrays into "unverified".
- C13 Citations: 112 now cites St John Ambulance (confirmed "999 or 112"); 000/112 cites healthdirect pages that say so (confirmed raw in the poisoning and triple zero pages); poisoncontrol.org relabelled.
- C14 Emergency list reworded; added NHS and AAP items confirmed raw.
- C15 Tap, do not shake (MedlinePlus), lights and unlock front door (healthdirect), cannot text 000 (healthdirect), jaw lift step conditional.

## Declined

- "Written next to the phone" address: declined. The raw AAP page says "At home keep your street address posted by the telephone", so the original was supported. Kept, reworded to "posted".
- Seizure: "unless the child has a seizure plan from their doctor": declined. It would weaken a call-now rule and no opened source says it.
- "Keep the child warm" for burns: declined. The NHS accident page says keep warm only when unsure whether to move a child, not for burns.
- "Cooling is not a reason to delay the call" and "have someone else call while you cool": not carried, no opened source states them.
- Anaphylaxis "if another adult is there, one calls while the other uses it": not carried as advice, no opened source states it. Moved to a clinician question.
- Canada 911 remote-area caveat and CRTC citation: declined. The CRTC page returned 403 or a security check; only a search snippet was seen. Listed in "unverified".
- Verifier's "poison.org tool for babies under 6 months" phrasing: the press release says phone is best for under 6 months; carried as that.
- Extras (home-access, button batteries for US readers): US button battery source not found, listed in "unverified".

## Still uncertain

No-phone lone-rescuer CPR timing (1 vs about 2 minutes), AHA original pages (403; used a copy of the AHA summary sheet on a university site), HRSA page (403), Canada 911 and poison hours, US button battery and magnet wording, seizure threshold, anaphylaxis positioning, consent and unreachable-parent wording.
