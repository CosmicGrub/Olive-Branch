# Corrections log round 2: high-fever (revision 3)

Each of R1 to R12 in VERIFY2-high-fever.md was re-checked on 2026-11-14 against the raw page text (curl download, then a script that strips markup), not against summaries. Pages read: AAP When to Call, AAP Fever and Your Baby, AAP Febrile Seizures, AAP KidsDoc fever checker, AAP How to Take Your Child's Temperature, NHS fever and NHS febrile seizures, RCH, Canadian Paediatric Society (fever and febrile seizures), Seattle Children's, Cleveland Clinic, MedlinePlus, Mayo content (Mosaic), CHOP and Poison Control.

## Applied (confirmed in the raw text)

- R1: confirmed. AAP When to Call lists all the listed signs under "call right away"; KidsDoc lists them under "call doctor or seek care now"; RCH, Mayo, NHS 111 and Seattle agree on the direction. The signs now sit in one new tier ("call the doctor or after-hours line right away"). "Usually walks and has stopped" was moved into the emergency "cannot walk" row so each sign has one tier only.
- R2: confirmed (NHS 999, MedlinePlus 911, Cleveland ER or 911, Mayo "right away" all list difficulty breathing without "severe"). The hedge is gone; KidsDoc and RCH differences are in a new disagreement block.
- R3 (boundary part): confirmed. AAP "Fever and Your Baby" says "3 months of age or younger"; AAP When to Call, KidsDoc, NHS, Mayo and MedlinePlus say younger than 3 months. A further AAP page (How to Take Your Child's Temperature) says "2 months old or younger"; shown as a position. The guide carries 3 months or younger, with non-overlapping bands.
- R4: confirmed. AAP temperature page: 1/2 inch under 6 months, up to 1 inch older; CPS: about 2.5 cm (1 inch), "only if a health care provider has shown you how". Both shown; new source 16.
- R5: applied with limits (see below).
- R6: applied with limits (see below).
- R7: confirmed. NHS 111 line for under 3 months and 3 to 6 months both end with "or you think they have a high temperature"; restored in the first emergency row and in the disagreement block.
- R8: confirmed (RCH "forehead infrared and plastic-tape thermometers can be unreliable"; CPS "head coverings, sweat, environment"). Note that the AAP temperature page says forehead thermometers can be used at any age, so this is shown as a difference.
- R9: confirmed. Cleveland has no clothing or keep-home advice and lists cool compresses as a treatment, so tag 8 was removed from those; Seattle supplies the dosing-device line; undress and bundle statements split.
- R10: applied.
- R11: confirmed on the raw page: CHOP lists cold hands and feet among normal fever symptoms (for otherwise healthy children over 3 months). Shown as a flagged difference; emergency placement kept (NHS 999); clinician question added.
- R12: applied as clinician questions only; all items re-found in the raw Cleveland and MedlinePlus text.

## Declined or changed

- R3 "up to about 13 weeks": not applied. No opened source states a weeks conversion for 3 months, so none is given. The guide instead says to count a baby who has just turned 3 months, and any baby whose exact age is unsure, in the high-risk band.
- R5 "give nothing to eat or drink": not applied (no authority opened says it). "Protect the head" (NHS only) and "wipe saliva outside the mouth" (CPS only) not applied (one authority each). Applied: on the side on a floor or bed, away from hard or sharp objects (AAP, NHS, CPS, Cleveland on side), do not hold down, nothing in the mouth, note the time (framed as: the emergency rules count minutes), keep on the side afterwards (NHS, CPS), then emergency care.
- R6 "if you cannot tell whether a sleeping child is breathing comfortably": not applied (no source). Applied: a sleeping child who cannot be woken or is very hard to wake is an emergency (KidsDoc, MedlinePlus, NHS).
- R2 optional disagreement line: applied as a full block, not a one-liner.
- R1 tier name: Cleveland Clinic lists dehydration, extreme drowsiness, continuous crying and "fever rash" under ER or 911, and MedlinePlus lists a new rash under 911. These were NOT put on the emergency list; they are in the right-away tier with the difference shown, and a clinician question asks whether to promote them. This is a judgement call, flagged.
