# VERIFY3: who-to-call, revision 3 (final independent regression check)

Date 2026-11-14. Read-only. Method: every cited page was re-fetched as raw HTML with curl and text-extracted with Python; the AHA sheet was read with pdftotext; Health Canada (curl could not connect) was read with WebFetch and a verbatim-quote prompt. The earlier verifiers, the corrector and the guide's own changelog were not trusted. No guide file was edited, no git was run. Scratch files are in the session scratchpad subfolder `v3wtc`. Pages not opened: HRSA, cpr.heart.org, CRTC, Australian Red Cross child page (same as the guide discloses).

**Overall verdict: NEEDS CORRECTIONS (small).** Every VERIFY2 correction was applied. No phone number is wrong, no dose is given, no step contradicts a major authority in a dangerous direction, JSON and Markdown are valid and in substance the same. Nothing is NOT SAFE. Three action-order wordings (severe bleeding, large-burn call, anaphylaxis with no auto-injector) are not yet one unambiguous rule, and a clinician should not have to find them. Fixing sections R1 to R3 below would make it READY FOR CLINICIAN REVIEW.

Counts: regression items 12 (10 fully applied, 2 partly); spot check 25 claims (20 SUPPORTED, 5 PARTLY SUPPORTED, 0 UNSUPPORTED, 0 CONTRADICTED); new/remaining corrections 15 (0 high, 3 medium, 12 low); corrector declines adjudicated 4 (1 reversed, 1 partly reversed, 2 upheld).

## 3. Remaining corrections with exact wording and sources

**R1 (medium). Severe bleeding first step order.** MD section 7 and JSON severeBleeding.firstStep say "Call emergency services and press firmly" ("call, then press"). SJA [27] puts pressure first for life-threatening bleeding; NHS [20] only says to call "if the bleeding is severe". Replace with: "Press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth at once and keep pressing; have someone call emergency services, or put the phone on speaker and call while you keep pressing. Leave stuck objects in and press on either side of them." Sources: SJA [27], NHS [19][20]. Also drop [20] from the "do not remove soaked cloth, add more on top" clause (NHS [20] says "replace it with a new one if possible"; NHS [19] says "apply another bandage or cloth on top"), or add one row to the disagreements.

**R2 (medium). Large, deep or face burn: order of call and cooling, and the face.** Burn firstStep ends with "Call emergency services for the signs above", after a 20-minute cooling instruction, so a lone adult can read it as cool for 20 minutes, then call. No opened source states the order (the guide says so in unverified). Do not carry a sourced claim; carry a safe guide wording marked as such: "For the signs above, emergency services are needed: ask someone else to call, or put the phone on speaker and call as you start cooling (guide wording, not stated by the sources)." Face burn: AAP [7] "For burns on the face, apply a cold wet washcloth. Do this right away." Add: "For a burn on the face, use a cool wet cloth on it rather than holding the face under running water (AAP symptom checker [7])." The 20-minute running-water rule is not workable for the face.

**R3 (medium). Anaphylaxis, one order and the no-injector case.** firstStep reads "If the child has their own prescribed epinephrine auto-injector, use it straight away ... and call emergency services at once". A reader with no injector can skip the call. Add as the first sentence: "Call emergency services at once for a suspected severe allergic reaction, even if there is no epinephrine (MedlinePlus [13]: 'Call 911 or the local emergency number immediately')." Then: "If the child has their own prescribed epinephrine auto-injector, use it straight away and then call (AAP [4], NHS [16])." Remove the sentence "AAP and NHS both put the injection first ... MedlinePlus lists the call first" from the advice string; it is already in the disagreement row. Also second dose trigger: AAP [4] says "symptoms get worse, continue, or do not get better in 5 minutes"; change "do not improve in 5 minutes" to "get worse or do not improve within 5 minutes".

**R4 (low). One burn call rule across sections.** Section 1 burn bullet lists large or deep, face, breathing trouble, smoke, difficulty waking (UK also electrical, chemical). Section 7 lists also hands, feet, genitals, joint, charred, numb, electrical, chemical for everyone. Section 4 says hand-sized, hands, feet, face, genitals or joint "should be seen immediately" (AAP [6] means paediatrician or ER, not necessarily 911). Pick the section 7 list (more cautious) and copy it into section 1, or label section 4 and section 7 differently ("seen immediately" versus "call emergency services").

**R5 (low). "Cannot speak".** Section 1 bullet 1 says "or the child cannot speak"; AAP [0] says "Trouble speaking". Use "has trouble speaking".

**R6 (low). Over-claim in the top clinician question.** MD Q1 says "With a phone, every opened authority says call on speaker first"; JSON reviewQuestions[0] says "all opened authorities". BRC [23] ("If you are on your own, call 999 after you've spent one minute") and MedlinePlus [12] do not mention a phone. Use: "With a phone, AHA, St John Ambulance and Resuscitation Council UK say call on speaker first and the American Red Cross lists the call first; British Red Cross and MedlinePlus do not mention a phone." The JSON disagreements[0].note ("every opened authority that handles the phone case") is accurate and can stay.

**R7 (low). Poison line fails to connect, child looks well.** The only fallback is "child is unwell or you believe the child is in danger, call 911". A well-looking child and a busy line has no rule. See section 4 on "redial". Suggested: "If the call does not connect, try again at once. If you still cannot get through, or the child is unwell, or you are worried, call 911 (guide wording; no opened source covers a failed connection)."

**R8 (low). Seizure attributed to the AAP ER list.** JSON urgentCareOrER[1]: "AAP ER list includes: ... seizure (call emergency services; see the seizure rule)". AAP [2] says seizure lasting more than 2 minutes in a child with no prior seizure. Change to: "... (AAP [2] lists only a seizure over 2 minutes with no prior history; this guide says call for any seizure, see section 9)".

**R9 (low). JSON/Markdown parity.** JSON only: "if you can safely do so" for a voice call (MD says flat "not a text"; AAP [1] says text may help if you cannot talk safely, voice call preferred); hazard lights if in a car; "if a mobile call drops, give your number" (AAP [1]); UK cut and broken-limb A&E lines (urgentCareOrER[4][5]); AAP ER list; electrical, charred, leathery or numb burns in the section 4 burn line; unverified item on a second adult calling, and the 33 lb nasal spray note. MD only: source [34] is never cited in the Markdown body (EU line has no bracket). Add to the MD or state that parity is by design.

**R10 (low). Source and status commentary still in advice strings.** callPoisonControl[0] ("Pages retrieved on 14 November 2026 ... The National Capital Poison Center closed ..."), MD section 3 bullet "US line status (resolved)", step 6 ("By region: US sources ... UK sources ..."), step 8 ("see disagreements for the 2-minute position"), burn firstStep ("the most cautious reading; see disagreements for 5, 10 and 20 to 30 minutes"), anaphylaxis firstStep ("see disagreements"). For a parent-facing string, keep one rule and move the commentary to the disagreement row. Keep the "2-minute position" pointer if the reviewer needs it.

**R11 (low). Seizure and the spinal exception.** The seizure first step says "if they are breathing, use the recovery position". Add "unless a neck or back injury is suspected (see step 9)", because the seizure list itself names "injured" and "in water". Source: SJA [28].

**R12 (low). Lone rescuer, spinal injury, no phone.** Step 9 says wait for help, step 8 says go to call. Neither covers a lone adult with no phone and a suspected spinal injury. One line for the clinician: "If you have no phone and no one else is there, shout for help; the clinician must decide the wording." Not a source gap to fix by guessing.

**R13 (low). Review date.** NHS [14] "When to call 999" shows "Page last reviewed: 3 February 2023, Next review due: 3 February 2026" (overdue at retrieval). It is used for location and callback items; the same items are in AAP [1] and healthdirect [32]. No wording change; note in the sources line.

**R14 (low). Section 1 "colour".** AAP [0] says "abnormal ABCs (appearance, breathing or circulation)"; the guide says "appearance, breathing or colour". Use "circulation (for example pale, mottled or cold)" or keep "colour" and add "circulation".

**R15 (low). Duplicated clinician question.** The 988 question appears in both the unverified list and the clinician questions with different wording. Fine, but the unverified entry should say it is a scope question, not a verification gap.

## 5. CPR "3 versus 2 authorities" count

Confirmed from raw text. One minute with no phone: St John Ambulance [25], British Red Cross [23], Resuscitation Council UK/ERC 2025 [42] (3). About two minutes: AHA 2025 Heartsaver sheet [39] (5 cycles of 30:2) and MedlinePlus [12] (2). The American Red Cross [40] is not on either side: its child steps put the 9-1-1 call before CPR and its "approximately 2 minutes" line is worded for a baby. Two caveats for the reviewer: (a) the three 1-minute sources are all UK or European and the two 2-minute sources are both US, so the split is regional as well as numerical; (b) BRC and MedlinePlus do not distinguish phone from no phone (R6). The guide carries 1 minute and shows 2 minutes: acceptable, because it is disclosed and the top question for the clinician.
