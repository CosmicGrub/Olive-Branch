## 2. Safety review

### P1 (most serious). Repeat vomiting is under-tiered
Cleveland Clinic lists "vomiting more than once" under 911 red flags. RCH fact sheet, healthdirect, PBB and CPS say go to hospital / emergency care for more than one vomit, and MedlinePlus says to get medical help right away. The draft's emergency list only says "keeps happening or will not stop", and "more than once" appears only in the same-day doctor list. A sitter could wait for a daytime appointment after two vomits. Only AAP says "more than 2 or 3". Safer: put "vomits more than once" in the go-to-emergency-department-now tier and note AAP's laxer number as a disagreement.

### P2. Tier mismatches against Seattle Children's "seek care now"
Seattle puts these in the NOW tier, not "same day": child under 1 year, unconscious under 1 minute, swelling over 2.5 cm, large dent, hard object, gaping cut, bleeding after 10 min pressure, and a cut with no tetanus shot at all. The draft's callDoctorSameDay list lists several of these. A parent reading "same day" for a gaping cut or an infant head injury is less cautious than the cited source. Rename that tier "seek care now or within hours" for these rows.

### P3. Missing or thin non-accidental injury (NAI) handling
The guide has no content saying that clinicians will ask careful questions and that it is routine and not an accusation. RCH clinician guide: consider abuse in all head injuries; consider it when the history does not adequately explain the injury. The AAP site says infants who cannot crawl rarely get bruises or broken bones, and an age-appropriate unwitnessed accident should not by itself raise suspicion (AAFP). The only content is the CDC shaking section. The clinician question asks for it but nothing is drafted. See corrections C7.

### P4. No hospital/ED vs GP separation for babies; baby 999 for any bruise
Fine and cautious, but the "same day" label on baby rows (S2) conflicts with the "call right away" text. Fix in P2.

### P5. Medicine
No dosing: good. But "ask a doctor, pharmacist or poison control" has no poison control number, and nothing says not to give medicines to babies without a doctor first. Seattle's 2-hour wait after vomiting dropped. AAP only says acetaminophen; a parent with only ibuprofen at home gets conflicting messages without being told which wins.

### P6. Neck and "roll to side"
Step 5 says to roll while step 3 says not to move. Both are what MedlinePlus says, but a lay reader needs the sentence "only roll them if they are vomiting or choking; keep head, neck and body in a line". Currently reasonably worded. Flag for the clinician; not a blocker.

### P7. "Keep them awake" myth
Not present anywhere. Draft correctly notes that no source says to keep awake. PASS.

### P8. Missing emergency/doctor signs that sources list
- AAP: "unusual paleness that lasts more than an hour", "looks sicker", "persistent ringing in the ears", "dizziness that doesn't go away or recurs" (dizziness is partly in watchFor).
- MedlinePlus: severe headache with stiff neck.
- NHS: condition that thins the blood or bleeding disorder, or taking blood thinners, 111; sick (vomiting) or dizzy 111.
- PBB: signs can show up to 3 days later (only partly used).
- CDC: "unusual behaviour" present; fine.
Loss of consciousness, vomiting, seizure, worsening headache, confusion/drowsiness, clear fluid, unequal pupils, weakness, bulging soft spot and the infant rule ARE all present.

## 3. Copyright review

No long copied passages. Closest paraphrases (consider rewording):
- "Do not shake a child who seems dazed, and do not pick up a fallen child who shows signs of a head injury" tracks MedlinePlus's do-not list ("Shake the person if they seem dazed", "Pick up a fallen child who shows any sign of head injury").
- "Do not pull out anything stuck in a wound, and do not wash a deep or heavily bleeding wound" tracks MedlinePlus ("Remove any object sticking out", "Wash a head wound that is deep or bleeding heavily").
- "Never leave a baby with someone who has a temper or a history of violence" tracks CDC ("easily irritated, has a temper, or has a history of violence").
- "If blood soaks through, add more cloth on top instead of lifting the first one" tracks MedlinePlus.
All are short, factual instructions. Low risk, but reword for the final text.

## 7. Overall verdict

NEEDS CORRECTIONS FIRST.

Reason: the sourcing is good and honest (no keep-awake myth, no dosing, clear disagreement section, no unopened sources used, all helplines verified). But the tiering of repeat vomiting and several "seek care now" items is less cautious than most cited authorities, the NAI handling is absent, the medicine wording cites a source that contradicts it, and a poison control number is missing. Apply C1-C9, then it is suitable to send to a clinician.
