Status: ready to send once the bracketed items are filled in. No personal details are in this file; the sender adds their own name and contact line. Written 2026-11-14.

## Part 1. The letter

**Subject: Please review five draft care guides for parents and carers (about an hour each; nothing is used until you sign off)**

Dear [Name],

I am building a small, private family app that helps separated and busy parents look after a child. One part of it, which we are calling "Care guides", is meant to give a parent, grandparent or sitter calm, plain steps for the moments that frighten people: a bump to the head, choking, a high fever, making the home safe, and who to call and what to say.

**What we are sending you.** Five draft guides, each in two forms (a readable file and the same content in a structured file), and a short review packet. Every step, threshold and phone number in the drafts was taken from an authoritative page that was opened and read (for example paediatric societies, Red Cross and heart associations, national health services, poison control services and children's hospitals). Each draft then went through independent checking and correction rounds; the logs are included. Where authorities disagree, the draft shows the disagreement and carries the most cautious reading we could defend. The drafts contain no medicine doses.

**What we need from you.** Your professional judgement on whether each guide is safe, correct and understandable to a frightened non-expert, and your decision on every point where the authorities disagree. The app has a rule that it ships no medical advice until a qualified clinician has reviewed it, guide by guide. **Nothing in these drafts is in the app, and nothing will be until you have signed off the guide.** If you decline or mark a guide unsafe, it stays out.

[For a paediatric clinician:] Please read the guides and answer the numbered questions for each one in the packet (53 in all across the five guides). [For a first-aid instructor, use part 3 instead.]

**How long it should take.** This is our estimate, not something we have timed with a reviewer. The packet is about 6,500 words and the five guides are roughly 7,000 to 11,000 words each. We suggest reading the packet first, then the guides in the order you prefer, spending the most time on the questions and on the "emergency first" section at the top of each guide. Plan on about one hour per guide, more for choking and for the fever guide, and you may review one guide at a time. A partial review of one guide is useful and welcome.

**What we cannot tell you.** Several official pages could not be opened and anything that rested only on a search snippet was left out and listed in each guide as unverified. Some rules rest on a single organisation. The guides are written for the United States first, with notes for the UK, Canada and Australia; please tell us which region your review covers. Teen content in the home-safety guide is thin. Nothing has been tested with real families.

**How to return your review.** Reply to this message with the checklist below filled in for each guide you reviewed (a typed reply or a marked-up copy is fine), and add comments in any form that suits you. For each guide please say one of: **Approved as written**, **Approved with the changes listed**, or **Not approved**. We will make the changes, send you the revised guide, and ask you to confirm it before it is used. If you are willing to be named as a reviewer in the app (for example "Reviewed by a paediatric clinician, [month year]"), please say so; naming is optional and we will not name you without your yes. [State here whether you can offer an honorarium, and the date you would like a reply by.]

Thank you. A careful no is as useful to us as a yes.

[Sender name]
[Contact line]

## Part 3. For the first-aid instructor (send in place of the clinician paragraph)

We are writing about two of the drafts, **Choking** and **Who to call and what to say** (which includes the order of calling and starting CPR for a lone sitter), and we would also value your reading of the **High fever** and **Bump to the head** emergency sections. The drafts say plainly that reading never replaces a hands-on first-aid and CPR course, and where the authorities differ on technique they say "follow the method taught in your course".

What we need from you: whether the **techniques as described** (back blows, abdominal and chest thrusts, hand positions and depth for babies and children, how CPR starts, what a lone sitter with no phone should do first) match what you teach and are safe for a person who has never trained; whether anything is easy to misread; and what you would add or remove. Several technique details were checked against raw page text because summary tools misreported at least one of them, but no instructor has seen them. Please use the same checklist (part 2) and tell us which course or body's method you teach, because that is the method we will name in the guide.
