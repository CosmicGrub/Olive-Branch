Welcoming people safely, and caring for this child

Two welcomes follow one rule. A scan is an introduction, never a key. And welcoming a person is a decision the family makes together, never something a newcomer can do to themselves. A person being limited is always the one who agreed to it. A sourced library of care guides, and a living "About Mia" profile the parents write, sit in the same Village. These are drafts of what could appear in the app: grown-ups in the olive theme, the child tablet in the child shell.

Adding a device

The PIN way is still the main way. The code is optional, for two devices in the same room. Both directions share the same Match, Approve and Settle-in steps.

Add a device → My family code → Child device scans → Match → Approve with PIN → Settle in

Welcoming a person

The extra layer. A new grown-up joins the village only when the grown-ups already there agree. Parents are equals; nobody can be brought in to push someone out.

Welcome someone → Pick who → Set limits → They join → The family votes → Welcomed, settling in

After the welcome

About Mia: the care profile

Every child is different, and only her parents know her tendencies, habits, allergies, medicines and diagnoses, on good days and hard days. This grows the existing care binder into one living profile that parents write, the right people read, and the child never sees. The app never advises: it shows what a parent typed, with their name and the date. Written as needs, never judgments.

Tell us about Mia → Keep her safe → What helps → Today's heads-up → Who sees what → The carer's view

Screenshots: a setting, and sharing between grown-ups only

System screenshots are off by default and are a per-phone setting for the grown-up who owns it. Sharing a screen is a separate, in-app action that can go only to another grown-up in the family, never to a child, and never through the phone's own share sheet. Sensitive screens are always blocked.

Care guides

What to do, and who to call, for anyone looking after the child. These screens show structure only. The content is being researched from authoritative sources and cannot appear in the app until a clinician has reviewed it. Every guide carries its sources, date and review status. Never shown to a child.

What a stranger can and can't do

AttemptWhat happens
Photographs a code and scans it from somewhere elseNot on the same network, so nothing happens.
Is in the room and scans itA request appears. The pictures won't match; "That's not mine"; the code is made new.
Scans a child's hello cardNothing is revealed. No request unless a grown-up opened adding.
Another family's app scans our code"This code belongs to a different family." Nothing is shared.
Uses an old invitation codeRefused, the same as a code that never existed.
Wins over one grown-up against the others' wishesA new parent needs all parents; a carer who can act needs all by default; a view-only person is limited, noticed by everyone and can be paused at once.
Tries to bring in a new adult to push out anotherCannot: new parents need every parent, and removal needs a visible notice and agreement.
