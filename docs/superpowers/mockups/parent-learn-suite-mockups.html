What the research links to it

Children tend to do well on school, mood and friendships when warmth and clear limits go together.

Correlation
What it does not say

Parenting does not simply cause outcomes. Children's temperament and genes also shape how adults respond. Studies of twins estimate about a quarter of the variation in parenting is child-driven.

Strong
Culture changes the meaning

A firm, high-expectation style can mean care and training in some families and coldness in others. Research found the same style predicting opposite school results in different communities.

9:41Time-out

A short, calm time-out

About one minute for each year of age. For a 6-year-old: about 6 minutes at most.

1Say what happened in plain words. "Hitting hurts."
2Take them to a boring, safe spot. Same place each time.
3Say nothing during it. Stay calm yourself. Take a breath.
4When it ends, reconnect. "Ready to try again?" The debt is paid.
5Catch them being good soon after. Be specific.
Not in the app, on purpose

Facing a wall for half an hour, punishing crying, early bedtime as a punishment. Pediatric guidance has moved away from these.

9:41Repair

After you lost your temper

It happens. What you do next matters more.

1Calm yourself first. Breath, water, step away safely.
2Go back and name it. "I yelled. That was not okay."
3Say it was yours, not theirs. "That was my big feeling."
4Reconnect. A hug if they want one. Then move on.
A line you can read aloud

"Mistakes happen. A mistake does not mean anyone is bad. We fix it and try again."

9:41Co-parenting

Keep your child out of the middle

Please don't

Use your child as a messenger. Question them about the other home. Criticise the other parent where they can hear. Ask them to keep secrets.

Try instead

Send messages parent to parent. If your child shares something, listen, then ask what they felt, not what the other parent did.

If your child says "Mum said you are…"

"I'm sorry that was hard to hear. We both love you. You don't have to carry that."

9:41Arguing

When you disagree in front of your child

Disagreement is normal. What children read is whether the family is still safe.

Mostly fine

Calm voices. Taking a break. Coming back to it. Working it out where they can see.

Hardest on children

Contempt, shouting, silent treatment, threats, or one of you walking out mid-fight.

1Pause. "We need a break. We'll talk later."
2Later, settle it away from them.
3Tell them. "We disagreed and we worked it out."

The child's side: nothing

The child shell has no settings and no adult content. This suite adds no screen, badge, tile or colour to it. A child never sees a style, a result, or a lesson meant for adults. The only way it touches the child is indirectly: a parent who has learned a calmer way to handle bedtime.

Evidence labels

Every card carries one. The label is the point of the suite: it teaches parents to ask "how sure are we?"

LabelMeansExamples in this suite
StrongMany studies, consistent direction, or a major body (e.g. the American Academy of Pediatrics) says it.Neglect harms. Physical punishment is ineffective and risks harm. Warmth plus clear limits is the best-supported pairing.
ModerateSeveral studies agree, but designs are limited or correlational.Consistency between caregivers helps. Cooperative co-parenting links to fewer problems.
WeakMostly small pilots or parent-reported results.Emotion coaching improving 5–7 year olds' emotion knowledge.
ContestedResearchers disagree, or it depends on culture.Whether "authoritarian" is harmful across cultures. Whether matching styles matters at all.

What we corrected from the PDFs

In the handoutIn the app
"Authoritative parenting: risks, none."Chip: "Not risk-free". Evidence is correlational and effects are modest.
Styles cause child outcomes."Linked to", never "produces". Child temperament and genes matter too.
Authoritarian is always harmful.Contested chip plus a culture note.
"Chairing": wall, 30 minutes, no crying, early bed.Not built. Replaced by a short calm time-out and "time-in".
"Good boy for…" praise cues.Specific praise prompts ("You shared the red block").
"These kids enjoy making you mad."Omitted. Assigning motives escalates conflict.

Rules it must keep

Never

Score, rank or label a parent.

Show a style or a reflection to the co-parent.

Offer an export of reflections (it could become dispute evidence).

Say a style causes an outcome.

Put any of this in the child shell.

Always

Show an evidence label and a source link.

Keep reflections private to the guardian.

Describe the day, not the person.

Offer a way to get support from a person (pediatrician or parent line).

Honest limit. The sources behind these cards were search excerpts and teaching handouts, not full papers. Before shipping any statistic, open the original study. This is education, not clinical advice.

Decisions that are yours

1. A Learn tab in the app, or keep this outside it?

2. Reflection: stored privately on the device, or session-only (nothing kept)? My lean is session-only. It removes any chance of it being used later.

3. Cultural framing default: a neutral "style is climate" frame with a culture note on every style card, or a short family-values question first?

4. The two-home rules list: wanted, or does it push toward comparing households? My lean is to build it, send-only, with no comparison.
