The Ugly Duckling

On a warm summer day, a mother duck sat on her nest of eggs, waiting for them to hatch. One by one, the eggs cracked open, and fluffy yellow ducklings peeped out into the sunshine. But one egg was bigger than the rest, and it took a little longer to open. When it finally did, out came a duckling who looked different from his brothers and sisters -- bigger, grayer, and not nearly as fluffy.

The other ducks on the farm did not know what to make of him. "He doesn't look like the rest of us," they said, and they were not kind about it. Some of the ducklings wouldn't let him swim near them at the pond. The hens clucked that he didn't belong. Even the old farm cat looked at him strangely. His mother tried to look out for him, but she could not be everywhere at once, and the little duckling began to feel very lonely. He didn't understand what he had done wrong -- only that he felt out of place wherever he went.

One evening, feeling sad and a little scared, the duckling decided to leave the farm and see if he might find somewhere he fit in better. He waddled through fields and hid among tall reeds, and though he sometimes felt afraid out in the wide world alone, he also felt a small flicker of hope every time he found a new place to rest. He met wild geese who flew off before he could really get to know them, and he spent a quiet, cold winter sheltering in a frozen marsh, doing his best to stay warm and safe until spring came again.

When spring finally arrived, the duckling had grown a great deal. One day he came upon a beautiful lake where three magnificent white swans were gliding across the water. He admired them from the reeds, thinking he had never seen anything so graceful, though he felt shy about swimming any closer -- surely, he thought, they wouldn't want plain, gray him around. But something made him step forward anyway, lowering his head, ready to be told to leave.

Instead, the swans swam toward him eagerly, as if greeting an old friend. Confused, the duckling looked down at his own reflection in the water for the first time in a long while -- and gasped. Looking back up at him was not a gray, awkward duckling at all. He had grown into a swan himself, with long elegant wings and feathers as white as clouds. He had been a swan all along; he had simply needed time to grow into who he really was.

Some children playing by the lake pointed and said, "Look, isn't he the loveliest swan of all?" And this time, when others looked at him, he did not feel embarrassed or out of place -- he felt proud, and happy, and completely at home. He spent the rest of his days swimming gracefully across that lake with his new swan family, and whenever he remembered the lonely days before, he felt only gentleness toward that small, sad duckling he used to be, glad that things had turned out so wonderfully in the end.
