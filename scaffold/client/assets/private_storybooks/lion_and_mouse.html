The Lion and the Mouse

Deep in a sunny stretch of the wild, a great lion lay stretched out on a warm rock, dozing after a big meal. He was the strongest animal for miles around, and every creature in the forest knew to walk softly and stay out of his way.

But a little mouse, out looking for seeds, didn't see the lion at all, not until she had already scurried right up his tail and across his paw. The lion woke with a roar and clamped his huge paw down around her before she could take another step.

"Please," squeaked the mouse, trembling but doing her best to sound brave, "I didn't mean to wake you. I was only looking for my breakfast. If you let me go, I promise you, someday I'll find a way to help you back."

The lion looked down at the tiny mouse in his paw and couldn't help but laugh. "You? Help me? What could someone so small possibly do for someone as big as me?" Still, her promise had a kind of courage to it that the lion admired, and besides, she was hardly a proper breakfast for a lion anyway. So he lifted his paw and let her scamper off into the grass.

Many days passed, and the lion mostly forgot about the little mouse. Then one evening, while walking through the trees, he stepped into a trap that hunters had hidden in the brush, a tangle of thick rope that pulled tight around him the moment he moved. He struggled and pulled and roared with all his strength, but the more he fought, the tighter the ropes seemed to hold him. He was truly stuck, with no way to free himself, and no one nearby to help.

His roars carried far across the forest, and it happened that the little mouse heard them. She recognized that voice, and she remembered her promise. She raced through the grass and the fallen leaves as fast as her small legs could go, until she found the lion tangled and worn out from struggling.

"Hold still," she told him, and she began to gnaw at the ropes with her sharp little teeth, one strand, then another, then another, working quickly while the lion, for once, did exactly as he was told. Bit by bit, the ropes came apart, until finally the lion could stretch his legs and stand up, free.

The lion looked at the mouse who had saved him, this creature he once thought too small to matter. "I laughed when you said you'd help me," he said. "I won't laugh at that again."

From then on, the two were unlikely friends, and the lion never forgot what he'd learned that day: that size isn't the only measure of what someone can give you, and that even the smallest kindness, offered honestly, can turn out to be exactly what saves you.
