The Boy Who Cried Wolf

There once was a boy whose job it was to watch over his family's sheep on the hillside every day. It was quiet work, and long, and after a while the boy grew bored of sitting alone with nothing but grass and sheep and sky for company.

One afternoon, just to see what would happen, he cupped his hands around his mouth and shouted down toward the village, "Wolf! Wolf! A wolf is after the sheep!"

Down in the village, people dropped what they were doing and came running with sticks and shouts, ready to chase the wolf away. But when they reached the hillside, they found no wolf at all, only the boy, laughing so hard he could barely stand. "You should have seen your faces," he said.

The villagers were not amused, but they went back to their work, and after a while, they nearly forgot about it.

Then, a few days later, the boy did it again. "Wolf! Wolf!" he cried, just for the fun of watching everyone come running. And once again, they hurried up the hill, and once again, there was no wolf, just the boy, giggling behind his sleeve.

This time the villagers were truly cross. "Don't call for us unless you mean it," one of them warned as they walked back down, shaking their heads.

Not long after that, the boy was watching his sheep as the sun began to sink low, when he heard a rustling in the bushes at the edge of the field. He turned, and there, stepping out from the shadows with its eyes fixed on the flock, was an actual wolf.

The boy's heart pounded. He jumped to his feet and screamed as loud as he could, "Wolf! Wolf! Please, somebody, a real wolf is here!"

But down in the village, no one moved. Someone glanced up at the hillside and said, "That's just him again, playing his trick," and they carried on with their supper. No one came, because no one believed him anymore.

The boy ran and shouted and waved his arms, trying to scare the wolf off by himself, but the sheep, frightened, scattered in every direction across the hillside and into the trees, and the wolf chased after them. By the time the boy finally reached anyone in the village who would listen, it was far too late to help. Many of the sheep were gone, run off into the wild hills, and some never came back at all.

The boy sat down in the grass that night, feeling sadder and more alone than he ever had before, and he understood, finally, what he had done. It wasn't the wolf that had cost him his flock. It was all those times before, when he'd asked people to trust him and given them a reason not to. Once he had used up their trust on jokes, there was none left for him when he truly needed it.

He never played that trick again. And it took a very long time before the people in the village believed him easily when he told them something was true, because trust, once it's broken, has to be built back up slowly, the same patient way it was built the first time.
