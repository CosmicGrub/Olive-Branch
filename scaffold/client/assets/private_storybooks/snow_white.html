Snow White

Long ago, a queen looked out at freshly fallen snow and wished for a little daughter with skin as soft and pale as snow, a gentle blush of pink in her cheeks, and hair as dark and glossy as a raven's wing. Before long, she had exactly the daughter she had wished for, and she named her Snow White. Sadly, the queen did not live to see her little girl grow up, and after some years the king married again -- this time to a woman who was strikingly beautiful, but also very proud, and who could not bear the idea of anyone being lovelier than she was.

The new queen owned a magic mirror, and often she stood before it and asked who was the most beautiful person in the whole land. For a long time the mirror always answered that she was. But as Snow White grew, she became more and more lovely, and one day the mirror had to admit that Snow White had become the fairest of all. The queen was so consumed with jealousy that she decided Snow White could not be allowed to stay at the palace any longer.

She asked a huntsman to lead Snow White deep into the forest and leave her there. But the huntsman had a soft heart, and when he saw how frightened and trusting Snow White looked, he could not bring himself to do anything unkind. Instead he told her to run as far as her legs would carry her and never look back. Snow White ran until she was breathless, deep among the trees, until she stumbled upon a cozy little cottage.

Inside, everything was built for someone small -- seven little chairs at the table, seven little beds in a row. It belonged to seven dwarfs who spent their days digging for jewels in the mountainside. When they came home to find a girl fast asleep across their beds, they were startled, but once she explained what had happened, they told her she was welcome to stay as long as she needed. Snow White cooked and tidied while they worked, and in the evenings they all shared stories by the fire. For the first time in a long while, she felt safe and cared for.

Meanwhile, back at the palace, the queen's mirror revealed that Snow White was not only alive, but still the fairest in the land. Determined to fix that, the queen disguised herself as a traveling peddler and made her way to the cottage, where she offered Snow White a rosy, perfect-looking apple. Snow White took a bite without knowing it was enchanted, and she immediately fell into a deep, unwakeable sleep.

When the dwarfs found her that evening, nothing they tried could rouse her. Heartbroken, they laid her gently in a bed made of glass, out in a quiet clearing where the sunlight could reach her, and they visited every day to keep her company. Birds nested nearby and deer grazed close by, as though standing guard over her.

Then one afternoon, a prince passed through the forest and saw her resting there. Something about her stillness moved him, and he came closer, and the instant he took her hand in his, the spell broke, and Snow White's eyes fluttered open.

When the queen learned that her scheme had failed and that Snow White was to marry the prince after all, she understood at last that her jealousy had cost her everything worth having. She left the palace that very night and was never seen in that kingdom again. Snow White, meanwhile, married her prince, stayed close with the seven friends who had looked after her, and lived a long, joyful life surrounded by people who loved her exactly as she was.
