The Tortoise and the Hare

Once upon a time, in a green meadow at the edge of a wood, there lived a hare who could run faster than anyone else around. He knew it, too, and he never missed a chance to say so. Whenever he passed the old tortoise plodding along the path, he'd zip past in a blur and call back over his shoulder, "Still moving, are you? At this rate you'll get where you're going sometime next year!"

The tortoise never got upset. She just kept walking at her own steady pace, one foot after another. But one day, tired of being teased, she looked up at the hare and said, "Since you're so certain you're faster than me, why don't we race and find out?"

The hare laughed so hard he nearly fell over. "You want to race me? All right. This should be quick."

So all the animals gathered at the edge of the meadow to watch. Someone marked a finish line far off at the old oak tree. At the signal, the hare shot off like an arrow, and the tortoise began her slow, even walk, one step, then another, then another.

Before long the hare was so far ahead that he couldn't even see the tortoise behind him anymore. He looked back, then looked at how much further there was to go, and thought, "There's no hurry at all. I could rest right here in this soft patch of clover and still win easily." So he curled up under a shady tree and closed his eyes, just for a little rest.

But a little rest turned into a long, deep sleep. While the hare snored away, the tortoise kept coming, slow and steady, never stopping, never speeding up, never slowing down. Step by step, she passed the very tree where the hare lay dreaming. She didn't wake him. She just kept going, because going was what she knew how to do.

When the hare finally opened his eyes, the sun had moved a long way across the sky. He stretched, yawned, and then his heart jumped. The tortoise was nowhere behind him. He looked ahead instead, and there she was, just a few steps from the old oak tree. He ran as fast as his legs would carry him, faster than he had ever run in his life, but it was too late. The tortoise reached the tree first, and all the animals cheered.

The hare sat down in the grass, out of breath and out of excuses. He had the faster legs, there was no question about that. But being fast hadn't been enough, not when he'd stopped trying. The tortoise, who never gave up and never let a little slowness make her quit, had won fair and square.

From then on, the hare still ran quickly when he needed to, but he never again napped in the middle of a race, and he never again teased anyone for going slow. He'd learned that steady, patient effort can carry you further than a fast start ever will, if you keep at it all the way to the end.
