The Velveteen Rabbit

One Christmas morning, a small boy woke to find a stuffed rabbit tucked into his stocking. The rabbit was made of soft brown velveteen, with pink lining in his ears and a bit of real fur for his tail, and for a little while he was the most exciting present of the morning. But once the newer, shinier toys came out to play, the velveteen rabbit was set on a shelf and mostly forgotten.

Living in the nursery already was an old Skin Horse, so worn that his coat had gone thin and one eye had been sewn back on crooked. He had seen many toys come and go, and the others thought him very wise. One quiet evening, the rabbit gathered his courage and asked him a question that had been sitting in his mind for weeks.

"What does it mean, to be Real?"

The Skin Horse thought about it for a while before he answered. "It isn't about what you're made of, or how you're put together," he said. "It happens when someone loves you for a long, long time, not just to play with, but truly loves you. Then, little by little, you become Real."

"Does it hurt?" asked the rabbit.

"Sometimes," said the Skin Horse. "But once you're Real, you can't be ugly, except to people who don't understand, and you can't go back to being unreal again. It lasts for always."

The rabbit thought about this for a long time afterward, and wondered if he would ever become Real himself.

He did not have to wonder for long. One night the boy couldn't find the toy he usually slept with, and his nurse, hunting in a hurry, grabbed the velveteen rabbit from the shelf and tucked him in beside him instead. From then on, the rabbit slept in the boy's bed every night, and all through the day the boy carried him everywhere: into the garden, down to the stream, out across the fields. He loved him so much that the velveteen wore thin in patches and most of the pink lining rubbed off his ears. The rabbit didn't mind, because somewhere in all that love, without ever quite noticing the moment it happened, he had become Real.

Then one summer, the boy grew very sick with a fever, and the whole house moved quietly and anxiously for many days. The rabbit stayed tucked under the covers the whole time, keeping him company, until at last the fever broke and the boy began to grow strong again.

But the doctor said everything the boy had held while ill needed to be cleared away and replaced with fresh things, to help him get properly well, and that included the little velveteen rabbit. Nobody had the heart to explain it to him directly. Instead, the rabbit was bundled up with some old blankets and set outside by the garden wall, to be taken away in the morning.

Out there alone in the grass, under the stars, the rabbit felt sadder than he ever had, missing the boy and wondering whether being Real was worth this kind of ache. But as he lay there, a small light glimmered among the flowers, and out of it stepped a gentle stranger who knelt down beside him.

"I am the nursery magic," she said kindly. "I look after toys that children have loved until they're Real, so they can be Real to everyone, always." She lifted the rabbit and kissed him, and in that moment he found he could move his own legs, and then he was hopping through the dewy grass on four soft feet, with fur and long ears all his own.

From then on, he lived at the edge of the wood behind the garden, and some evenings he crept close to the house to watch the boy playing outside. The boy never guessed that the wild rabbit watching from the hedge was the very one he had once loved to pieces. But the little rabbit knew, and he was happy, because he had learned that love, even after it seems to end, has a way of finding you again in some new and unexpected shape.
