The Tale of Peter Rabbit

Once upon a time, in a sandy burrow underneath the roots of a big fir tree, there lived a mother rabbit and her four little ones: Flopsy, Mopsy, Cottontail, and Peter.

Every morning, Mother Rabbit smoothed their fur and sent them out to play among the ferns and blackberry brambles. But before they scampered off, she always gave the same gentle reminder. "You may go anywhere you like today, my dears, except into Mr. McGregor's garden. Your father had a terrible fright in there once, and I don't want the same thing happening to you. Off you go now, and mind you behave."

Flopsy, Mopsy, and Cottontail were good little rabbits. They hopped down the lane to gather blackberries, their little tails bobbing like puffs of cotton.

But Peter was curious, and curious rabbits sometimes forget to listen. Instead of following his sisters, he squeezed under the gate into Mr. McGregor's garden.

Oh, what a garden it was! Peter had never seen so many good things to eat. He nibbled lettuces and French beans, and he crunched on radishes until his whiskers were dusted with dirt. He was just starting to feel a little too full when he came around a cucumber frame and found himself nose to nose with Mr. McGregor, who was down on his knees planting cabbages.

"Stop, thief!" shouted Mr. McGregor, jumping up.

Peter's heart went thump-thump-thump. He tore off across the garden, losing one shoe among the potatoes and the other in the cabbages, and went bounding along on four legs as fast as he could go.

Then, worse luck, he ran straight into a gooseberry net and got stuck fast by the buttons of his little blue jacket. He wriggled and squirmed while some friendly sparrows fluttered down and chirped at him to keep trying, keep trying, until, with one great tug, Peter burst free. He left his jacket behind on the net and dashed on in just his shirt.

He hid, shaking, inside a toolshed for a moment, but a watering can nearby gave a suspicious rustle, and he decided not to wait around to find out why. He crept past a large white cat sitting very still beside a pond (and every rabbit knows to be wary of a cat that sits too still), and at last, far across the garden, he spotted the very gate he'd come in by.

Mr. McGregor saw him running and gave chase, but Peter was small and quick and knew how to duck under things that grown-ups couldn't. He scrambled beneath the gate with Mr. McGregor's boots stomping close behind, and he didn't stop running until he was safely home in the soft, dark burrow.

Peter was so out of breath he could hardly speak. His mother took one look at him, missing his shoes, missing his jacket, and instead of scolding him, she simply gathered him up in her arms.

"Oh, Peter," she said softly, smoothing his ears back into place. "What an adventure you've had."

She tucked him into bed and made him a warm cup of chamomile tea, while Flopsy, Mopsy, and Cottontail had bread and milk and blackberries for their supper. Peter didn't mind missing the blackberries one bit. He was safe and warm and loved, curled up right where he belonged, and he decided that next time, he might just listen to his mother after all.
