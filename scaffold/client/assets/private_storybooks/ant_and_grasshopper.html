The Ant and the Grasshopper

All through the long, warm summer, a little ant worked without stopping. Every day she marched back and forth across the field, carrying seeds and crumbs of food, one after another, tucking them away safely in her home underground. It was hard work, and slow, and the sun was often hot on her back.

Nearby, in the tall grass, a grasshopper spent his summer a very different way. He hopped from stem to stem, played his fiddle in the sunshine, and sang songs to anyone who would listen. Watching the ant trudge by with yet another seed on her back, he called out, "Come play with me! Summer is for singing, not all this working. Look how much of it you're wasting!"

"I'm putting food away for winter," the ant answered, without slowing down. "You might want to do the same."

The grasshopper laughed and waved a leg. "Winter is so far off. Why worry about it now, when the sun is warm and the music is good?" And he went back to his fiddle, while the ant went back to her work.

This went on all summer long, the ant gathering and storing, the grasshopper singing and playing, each of them quite certain they had the better idea.

Then the days grew shorter, and the wind turned cold, and the leaves fell from the trees, and winter came to the field the way it always does. Snow covered the grass. The stems the grasshopper used to hop between were buried and bare. His fiddle had nothing to play for anymore, because there was no one left outside to hear it, and worse than that, there was no food anywhere to be found. He grew colder, and hungrier, and more tired with each passing day, until at last, shivering, he made his way to the little door in the ground that led to the ant's home, and knocked.

The ant opened her door to find the grasshopper standing there, thin and shivering, all his summer music gone quiet. "I didn't put anything away," he admitted quietly. "I didn't think winter would really come."

The ant looked at him for a moment. She remembered the hot days of summer, and her sore feet, and all those seeds she'd carried one by one while he played his songs. She could have reminded him of everything she'd said back then. Instead, she stepped aside and said, "Come in and warm up. There's enough here to share, at least for tonight."

She fed him and let him rest by the warmth of her store of food, but she also told him plainly, "This won't last forever, and I won't always be able to share. Next summer, you'll need to do your own gathering too."

The grasshopper nodded, grateful and a little ashamed, and he never forgot that winter, how hungry and how afraid he'd felt, and how it was the ant's hard work, and then her kindness, that had gotten him through it. When summer came again, he still sang, because singing was who he was, but he gathered too, so that the next winter, he wouldn't have to knock on anyone's door.
