Cinderella

Once there was a kind girl named Cinderella who lived with her stepmother and two stepsisters. After her father died, the stepsisters were not very nice to her. They kept the pretty room for themselves and gave Cinderella a small spot by the fireplace to sleep, and they asked her to do all the sweeping and cooking and mending. Cinderella did the work without complaining, but sometimes, when everyone else was asleep, she looked out the window at the stars and wished her life could be a little different.

One day a letter arrived from the palace. The king was holding a grand ball, and every young woman in the kingdom was invited, so that the prince might find someone to share his life with. Cinderella's stepsisters squealed with excitement and spent days trying on gowns and jewelry. Cinderella offered to help pin their hair and hem their skirts, all the while quietly hoping that maybe, just maybe, she might get to go too. But when the night of the ball arrived, the stepsisters swept out the door in their finery and left Cinderella behind without a second glance.

Cinderella sat by the fire and let herself cry, just a little. Then, in a shimmer of light, a kind old woman appeared in the kitchen. "I'm your fairy godmother," she said, "and there's no reason at all you should miss the ball." She tapped a pumpkin from the garden with her wand, and it turned into a golden carriage. She tapped six mice, and they became six white horses. With one more tap, Cinderella's worn dress became a gown the color of moonlight, and on her feet appeared two slippers made of glass, so clear they looked carved from starlight.

"Enjoy every minute," said the fairy godmother, "but remember -- the magic fades the moment the clock strikes midnight."

At the ball, nobody recognized Cinderella in her beautiful gown. The prince asked her to dance, and they danced together all evening, laughing and talking as though they'd always known each other. Cinderella was having such a wonderful time that she nearly forgot the hour, until she heard the clock beginning to chime midnight. She turned and ran, right down the palace steps, and in her hurry, one glass slipper slipped from her foot and was left behind.

The prince picked up the slipper and announced that he would marry whoever it fit. He and his servants traveled from house to house across the kingdom, until at last they reached Cinderella's home. Both stepsisters tried hard to squeeze their feet into the tiny glass slipper, but it simply would not fit, no matter how they wiggled their toes. Then Cinderella asked if she might try, and although her stepsisters laughed, the prince said everyone deserved a turn. The slipper slid onto her foot perfectly, as though it had been made for her -- because it had.

The prince recognized her at once as his partner from the ball, and his whole face lit up. Cinderella married the prince, and being as good-hearted as ever, she forgave her stepsisters and invited them to visit often. From then on, her days were full of the kindness she had always given so freely to others, and she and the prince lived happily for many years to come.
