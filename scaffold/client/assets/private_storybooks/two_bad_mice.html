The Tale of Two Bad Mice

In a cozy dollhouse that stood in the corner of a nursery lived two dolls named Lucinda and Jane. Lucinda was very grand and hardly ever said anything, and Jane was supposed to be the cook, though truthfully she never cooked a single thing, because the dollhouse kitchen was already stocked with the most beautiful pretend food anyone ever saw: a shiny ham, a plump fish, a bowl of pears, and a whole pudding, every bit of it painted and polished to perfection and none of it real in the slightest.

One quiet afternoon, while the little girl who owned the dollhouse was out and the two dolls sat stiffly in their chairs by the fire, two mice came creeping out from a hole behind the skirting board. Their names were Tom Thumb and his wife, Hunca Munca, and they were terribly curious about the grand little house that had suddenly appeared in their nursery.

"Just look at that," whispered Tom Thumb, peeking in through the dollhouse door. "A whole feast, and not a soul eating it. Shall we go in and see?"

In they crept, and the dining room was more splendid than anything they had ever seen: a polished little table set with tiny plates, and upon it, the ham, the fish, and all that lovely food. Tom Thumb snatched up the ham right away and took an enormous bite.

Nothing happened. He bit again, harder this time. Still nothing. The ham was hard as a stone, because of course it was only paint and plaster, made to look delicious and never meant to be eaten by anybody.

Tom Thumb was so cross that he flung the ham straight onto the floor, where it bounced and clattered away. Hunca Munca tried the fish next, and then a pear, and then the pudding, and every single thing turned out to be just as hard and just as fake as the last.

Well, that was the end of their patience. If they couldn't eat the food, they decided, they would simply have some fun instead. Tom Thumb scrambled up the chimney to see whether it was a real one (it was not, and he came back down covered head to toe in soot). Meanwhile, Hunca Munca discovered a little cupboard full of tiny dishes and, in all her excitement, knocked half of them straight onto the floor, where they smashed into pieces.

Then the two mice had a wonderful idea. Since the dollhouse had so many fine things and didn't seem to need any of them, they decided to borrow a few treasures for their own mouse hole: a little cradle, a book or two, a tiny birdcage, and a couple of small chairs, plus anything else they could carry. Tom Thumb even wrestled a soft feather cushion down into their hole, huffing and puffing until it finally squeezed through.

Just as they were finishing their work, they heard footsteps and voices coming closer, and the two mice scampered back to their hole as fast as their little legs could carry them, hearts thumping with the thrill of a narrow escape.

When the little girl came home and found her dollhouse all topsy-turvy, with broken dishes and missing treasures, she was quite puzzled indeed, and a toy policeman doll was even brought in to look into the matter, though of course he never turned up a single clue.

But Hunca Munca did feel a bit sorry, in her own small mousy way. That very night, once the whole nursery was fast asleep, she crept back with a tiny broom and swept the dollhouse floor as clean and tidy as she possibly could, humming happily to herself the entire time.

And ever since, on nights when the nursery is especially quiet, if you listen very closely, you might just hear the soft pitter-patter of a broom sweeping -- because some say that Hunca Munca still comes back now and then, just to tidy up the mess her curiosity once made.
