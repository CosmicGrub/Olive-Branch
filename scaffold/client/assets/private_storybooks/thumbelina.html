Thumbelina

Once there was a woman who wished very much for a child, so she visited a kind old woman who gave her a magic seed to plant. She planted it in a pot, and it grew into a beautiful flower that looked like a tulip about to bloom. When the petals opened, there inside sat a tiny girl, no bigger than a thumb, and so the woman named her Thumbelina. Thumbelina slept in a walnut shell lined with soft petals and spent her days sailing a flower petal like a little boat across a bowl of water, and she was loved very much.

One night, a toad hopped in through the open window and saw Thumbelina sleeping. She thought Thumbelina would make a lovely friend for her son, so she scooped her up, walnut shell and all, and carried her down to the marsh without asking. When Thumbelina woke up on a lily pad, surrounded by water on every side, she felt scared and terribly far from home, and she cried because she didn't want to stay there at all -- she just wanted to go back to her own house. Luckily, some kind fish nearby heard her crying and felt sorry for her. They nibbled through the stem of the lily pad so it floated free, carrying Thumbelina safely away down the stream, out of reach of the toad and her son.

Thumbelina drifted along on her lily pad for some time, watching dragonflies skim the water in the sunshine, until a large beetle swooped down, picked her up, and carried her off to a tree to show her to his friends. They looked her over and decided she wasn't beetle-like enough for their taste, which hurt her feelings, and before long the beetle set her back down in a meadow and flew off without her. Thumbelina was alone once more, but she did her best, building herself a little bed of leaves and grass, and getting through the summer on nectar and seeds.

When autumn turned to winter, Thumbelina grew cold and hungry, and she was very relieved when a kind field mouse invited her to shelter underground for the season. The mouse was warm and welcoming, though she often talked about how nice it would be if Thumbelina married the mouse's neighbor, a wealthy mole who lived nearby. The mole was pleasant enough, but he never went outside and cared nothing for sunshine or flowers, and Thumbelina realized, quietly, that his cozy tunnel was not where she wanted to spend her life -- she missed the sky far too much.

While exploring the mole's tunnels, Thumbelina found an injured swallow who had grown too weak to fly south. She nursed him back to health all winter, keeping him warm and telling him stories, and when spring came, the swallow was strong enough to fly again. Grateful, he asked her to climb onto his back and see where his wings might take her. They flew over forests and rivers until they reached a sunny meadow full of flowers, and there, to her delight, Thumbelina discovered tiny people just her own size, with wings as delicate as her own dreams. Their gentle prince took one look at her and knew at once that she belonged among them.

Thumbelina finally understood why she had never quite fit in anywhere before -- she simply hadn't found her true home yet. She stayed in that meadow with her new friends, was given a pair of gossamer wings of her own, and from then on she spent her days flying from flower to flower in the sunshine, exactly where she was always meant to be.
