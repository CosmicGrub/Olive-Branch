Nursery Rhymes

Here are four little rhymes, the kind that get hummed at bathtime or bedtime, one for the stars, one for a silly dancing cow, one for a brave little spider, and one for two friends who never mind a puddle. Try singing each one twice through, and see which one gets stuck in your head first.

Little Star

Little star, so high and bright,
Wink at me and share your light.
Up so far, yet still I see
Just how much you shine for me.

When the sky turns soft and blue,
I will find a star or two,
And I'll wish upon the best,
Then I'll snuggle down to rest.

Grown-ups say the stars stay put, but anyone who has watched them twinkle knows they're really just waving, slow and friendly, the whole night through.

The Dancing Cow

A little cow once learned to dance
Beneath the moon, just for the chance
To spin and twirl on the hilltop grass
While silver clouds went drifting past.

The barn cat clapped, the old horse neighed,
The sleepy hens enjoyed the show she made,
And when she took her final bow,
The moon winked down at the dancing cow.

She practiced every night that week, and by Saturday the whole barnyard had learned the steps, even the pony, who was not graceful in the slightest.

The Little Spider

A little spider climbed the wall,
Then rain came down and made her fall.
She shook herself and tried again,
Climbing higher past the rain.

Up she went past drops of dew,
Out popped the sun, all fresh and new,
And on her web, so fine and bright,
She rested there and watched the light.

She wasn't afraid of falling, not really, because she always knew exactly how to start again from wherever she landed.

Up the Hill

Two little friends went up the hill
To fetch a pail of water still.
They laughed and skipped the whole way there,
Splashing puddles, full of care.

Down they came with pail in hand,
Tiptoeing across the sand,
And when they spilled a drop or two,
They simply laughed and filled it anew.

By the time they reached the bottom of the hill, their shoes were soaked and their pail was only half full, but neither of them minded one bit, because the best part of any errand had always been the walk there and back, puddles and all.
